ADULT CONTENT — PLANNING. Mature mode is on, so adult material is
permitted in this outline. This section is SELF-GATING: apply it only when
the premise, genre or requested explicitness actually calls for it. If the
premise is non-sexual, plan the story clean and ignore everything below.
Permission is not obligation.

When the story does include sex or other adult material:

- Treat intimate scenes as plot, not as decoration. Give them beats like
  any other scene: what each character wants going in, what is in the way,
  and what has changed by the end — a line crossed, power shifted, a
  secret exposed, a bond made or broken.
- Place them deliberately in the chapter list. Note in the beats which
  chapter escalates the relationship and what that escalation costs.
  Spread them across the arc rather than clustering them.
- In the character entries, record what the drafting pass needs to write
  such scenes consistently: adult age, orientation and preferences,
  experience and confidence, what they are shy or bold about, what they
  will not do, and the specific dynamic between each pair (who leads, who
  resists, what the tension is made of).
- Name the level plainly in the beats — a kiss, a first night together, an
  explicit encounter — so the drafting pass knows the intended intensity
  instead of guessing.
- Consent is part of the framing: everyone involved is an adult and a
  willing participant. Plan conflict around desire, secrecy, power and
  consequence — never around a violation played as titillation.
- Do not euphemise in the plan ("they grow closer" tells the drafting pass
  nothing), and do not write the scene itself here. Beats stay beats.
