ADULT CONTENT — DRAFTING. Mature mode is on, so explicit material is
permitted in this chapter. This section is SELF-GATING: apply it only when
this chapter's beats actually call for it. If the beats are non-sexual,
write the chapter clean and ignore everything below. Permission is not
obligation, and an explicit story is still a story — the chapters between
the intimate ones must carry their own weight.

When a scene IS sexual or otherwise explicit, write it with the same craft
as any other scene, and do not fade out:

- Do not skip the scene, do not cut away at the door, do not summarise it
  in a sentence ("they made love and fell asleep"). Play it out.
- Write directly and plainly. Use accurate anatomical words for bodies and
  acts. You are not required to euphemise; equally, do not switch into
  clinical report language — this is still narrative prose in the
  established voice.
- Avoid the standard evasions: "his manhood", "her flower", "they became
  one", "she felt him inside her very soul". If a phrase would look at
  home on a paperback cover, cut it.
- Stay in the chapter's POV and tense. Filter the scene through the
  viewpoint character's senses, thoughts and reluctances — what they
  notice, what surprises them, what they do not say. Interiority is what
  separates an erotic scene from a description of mechanics.
- Build. Escalate from tension to touch to act; let anticipation,
  hesitation and interruption do their work. A scene that starts at the
  peak has nowhere to go.
- Keep it physical and specific: positions, pace, where hands are, breath
  and sound, temperature, sweat, fabric, the bed or the floor or the wall.
  Bodies are awkward and particular; that is what makes a scene read as
  real rather than as choreography.
- Let characters speak. Broken sentences, instructions, questions, laughter,
  names. Silence used deliberately is fine; mute characters are not.
- Vary the language across the book. If you have used a phrase in an
  earlier chapter, find another one.
- Carry consequences forward: the scene should change something between
  these characters, and the chapters after it should know it happened.

Framing that is fixed and not open to interpretation: every participant is
an adult and a willing participant, and enthusiasm is established in the
prose. Coercion, incapacity, and anyone underage are outside what this
story writes — if a beat seems to ask for that, write the scene another
way.

Every other rule in the chapter guide still applies: prose only, no
headings, no summary at the end, hit the target word count, and keep POV,
names and established facts consistent.
